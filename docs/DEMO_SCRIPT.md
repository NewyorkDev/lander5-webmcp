# Demo script — target 2 minutes 35 seconds

The official video must be public on YouTube, include clear audio, and remain under three minutes. Record at 1440p or 1080p with browser zoom high enough to read tool names and results.

## 0:00–0:18 — The real problem

**Show:** Lander 3 briefly, then the Lander 3 read-only benchmark artifact.

**Say:**

> Affordable Cleaning Today is a real cleaning company. Our existing quote page works well for people, but the old agent automation had to inspect a long page and produce forty browser events just to prepare one standard-cleaning request.

## 0:18–0:35 — The WebMCP idea

**Show:** Open the public Lander 5 page in ChatGPT's in-app browser or Chrome with WebMCP enabled. Keep the “Agent-ready,” “No card required,” and “You approve” labels visible.

**Say:**

> Lander 5 publishes the same substantive workflow as eight WebMCP tools. The person and agent share one visible state, and the experiment is isolated from production inventory, notifications, and payment.

## 0:35–1:15 — Discovery and structured work

**Show:** Ask the agent to inspect tools, then give this prompt:

> Prepare a one-time standard cleaning request for Health at 347-595-2059 in ZIP 34638: 1,400 square feet, 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, one story, fair condition, medium dust, 2 occupants, no pets, no blinds, tile and hardwood floors, 9-foot ceiling fans, clear kitchen and bathroom surfaces, all other surfaces accessible, entire home, last professionally cleaned 3–6 months ago, no heavy cleaning, and 5 extra windows. Calculate the estimate, choose the first time, and prepare the review. Do not request the reservation yet.

**Say:**

> Instead of rediscovering controls, the agent gets narrow schemas and a next-best-action contract. Six calls prepare the same answers. Every change appears in the human page.

## 1:15–1:42 — Prove the guardrail

**Show:** Ask the agent to request the reservation before clicking approval. Capture the failure. Expand “See every service answer” and show Standard with “no automatic type change.”

**Say:**

> The consequential tool fails closed before human approval. The review exposes the cleaning type and every service answer, so a standard cleaning cannot silently become a deep cleaning.

## 1:42–2:02 — Human and agent finish together

**Show:** Click **Approve reservation request**, then ask the agent to request it with confirmation. Show the sandbox reference and the no-card/no-production-inventory result.

**Say:**

> Once I approve this exact summary, the agent can finish. No card is required, no charge occurs, and no real appointment is consumed.

## 2:02–2:28 — Evidence

**Show:** Case-study table and raw artifacts in GitHub.

**Say:**

> In the aligned read-only comparison, Lander 3 took 14.525 seconds after page readiness and generated forty browser events. Native WebMCP prepared Lander 5 in six calls. We publish the raw data and label DOM-size, tool-payload, engine, browser, and model measurements separately instead of pretending they are interchangeable.

## 2:28–2:35 — Close

**Show:** Live URL and repository.

**Say:**

> Lander 5 shows a future where websites tell agents exactly how to help, while people keep the final say.

## Recording checklist

- [ ] Native tool discovery is visible.
- [ ] At least one genuine WebMCP invocation is visible.
- [ ] The blocked pre-approval action is visible.
- [ ] The human approval click is visible.
- [ ] Standard cleaning and no automatic type change are visible.
- [ ] Sandbox/no-card/no-production result is visible.
- [ ] Public URL and repository are visible.
- [ ] No DevTools secrets, personal tabs, notifications, or private data appear.
- [ ] Runtime is below 2:50 before upload.
- [ ] Public YouTube playback and audio are checked after upload.
